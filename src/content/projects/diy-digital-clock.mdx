## Motivation

A from-scratch digital clock to get comfortable with the full stack: schematic, PCB layout, firmware, and debugging on real hardware — not a shield or a module breakout.

## Key Components

- **ATmega328P** — runs the display multiplexing and I²C communication
- **DS1307 RTC** — keeps accurate time with a coin-cell backup
- **4-digit 7-segment display** — multiplexed via the ATmega, one digit active at a time

## Challenges & How They Were Solved

**I²C communication issues** — The DS1307 initially returned corrupted data. Root cause was missing pull-up resistors on SDA/SCL (left floating). Adding 4.7 kΩ pull-ups to 5 V resolved the issue immediately.

**Display ghosting** — At the initial multiplexing timing, segments from the previous digit were faintly visible when switching. Fixed by inserting a short blanking interval between digit switches, turning all segments off for ~50 µs before activating the next digit.

**Brightness balancing** — Different digit positions appeared at different brightness levels due to unequal parasitic loading. Adjusted the current-limiting resistors per digit to normalize perceived brightness across all four positions.

**Clock drift** — The DS1307's 32.768 kHz crystal had measurable drift over 24 hours. Compensated in firmware by applying a per-day correction offset measured against a reference clock over one week.

## Outcome

Clock runs accurately in daily use, correct time maintained through power cycles via the coin-cell backup.
