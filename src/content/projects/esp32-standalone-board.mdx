## Objective

A compact, battery-powered ESP32 board for development and deployment. It combines an ESP32-WROOM-32-N4 module, a USB-C Li-ion charger, a 3.3 V buck-boost regulator, a 6-pin auto-reset programming header and a set of 2.5 mm XH breakout connectors on a single PCB.

The board is meant as a standalone ESP32 node: plug in a battery, switch on, and use the connectors to attach I²C sensors, SPI or UART peripherals and other GPIO-driven modules.

## Key Components

| Component | Ref | Role |
|---|---|---|
| ESP32-WROOM-32-N4 | U4 | Dual-core microcontroller module with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and 4 MB flash |
| BQ24092DGQR | U3 | Li-ion charger, current set by R13 and limited to the USB 500 mA level, 4.20 V |
| TPS63020DSJT | U2 | Buck-boost regulator, 3.28 V set by R11/R12 |
| XFL4020-152MEC | L1 | 1.5 µH buck-boost inductor |
| 0603L100SLYR | U5 | 1 A resettable PTC fuse on USB VBUS |
| BC847 | Q3, Q4 | Automatic reset and boot-mode circuit |
| PZ254V-11-06P | H1 | 6-pin programming header for a 3.3 V USB-UART adapter |
| TYPE-C 6P(073) | USB1 | Power-only USB-C input with 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs |
| SK12D07VG3 | SW1 | Power switch between VBAT and VIN |
| ZX-XH2.54-4PZZ | CN1, CN3, CN4, CN6, CN7 | 4-pin XH connectors for I²C, spare GPIOs and 3.3 V out |
| ZX-XH2.54-2PZZ | CN2 | Battery connector on the bottom side |
| KT-0603R | LED2 | Red LED on the charger PG pin, lit while USB power is present |
| Passives | R6–R20, C5–C18 | Pull-ups, feedback and charger setting, battery divider, decoupling |

## Features

- ESP32-WROOM-32-N4 with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth; the PCB antenna overhangs the top edge by about 5.6 mm.
- 1-cell Li-ion/Li-Po on CN2, charged from USB-C by the BQ24092 at the USB 500 mA level, with 6.6 V input over-voltage protection.
- TPS63020 buck-boost regulator producing 3.28 V above and below the battery voltage.
- 1 A resettable PTC fuse on USB VBUS and 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs for USB-C sink operation.
- 6-pin programming header with a two-BC847 DTR/RTS auto-reset circuit, so uploads need no buttons.
- I²C port on CN7 (GPIO21/22) and spare GPIOs on CN1 (VSPI pins), CN3 and CN4, plus 3.3 V output on CN6.
- Battery sense divider (R19/R20, 100 kΩ each) to GPIO34, reading the cell while SW1 is on.
- Slide switch between battery and regulator; the cell still charges when the board is off.
- Four plated M3 mounting holes on GND.

## Hardware Design

### Power

USB-C 5 V passes a 1 A PTC fuse to the BQ24092 charger, which charges the cell on CN2. SW1 connects the battery to the TPS63020, whose 1 MΩ / 180 kΩ feedback divider sets 3.28 V for the ESP32 and the CN6 and CN7 outputs. The USB-C port carries power only, with no data lines.

### Microcontroller and programming

Header H1 brings out DTR, TXD, RXD, 3V3, RTS and GND for a 3.3 V USB-UART adapter. BC847 transistors Q3 and Q4 drive GPIO0 and EN from DTR and RTS, giving automatic reset and download mode from the Arduino IDE, PlatformIO or ESP-IDF.

### Expansion

CN1 carries GPIO5, 18, 19 and 23 (VSPI), CN3 carries GPIO35, 25, 26 and 27, and CN4 carries GPIO14, 13 and GPIO2 through a 220 Ω series resistor. CN7 is the I²C port; it has no pull-ups on the board, so attached devices bring their own.

### PCB

The board is 62.9 × 33.0 mm, 2 layers, 1.6 mm thick, with 37 SMD placements on top and 9 hand-soldered parts.

## Outcome

- Full fabrication package in the GitHub repo: three-sheet schematic PDF and PNG exports, EasyEDA Pro project, Gerbers, BOM with LCSC numbers, pick-and-place file and STEP model.
- Interactive HTML documentation covering every circuit block with its schematic, simulations, an ESP32 pin map, a board status preview and a 3D model made from the STEP file.
- No firmware is included; the board is a general-purpose ESP32 platform programmed through H1.
- Known V1.0 issue documented for the next revision: the TPS63020 EN, VINA and PS/SYNC pins are not connected to VIN, so the 3.3 V rail is not expected to start. A one-wire rework for built boards is described.
- Further recommended changes include fixing the charger TS pin, adding a capacitor on the charger output, battery reverse-polarity protection, RESET/BOOT buttons and I²C pull-ups, and routing the charger status pins to free GPIOs.
