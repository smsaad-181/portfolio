## Objective

An ESP32-based patient vitals monitor that reads an ECG waveform and heart rate, blood oxygen (SpO₂), body temperature and room conditions, and presents them on a live web dashboard. The dashboard is served straight from the ESP32 over Wi-Fi, so no cloud service, phone app or external server is involved.

The hardware is a custom through-hole carrier PCB that brings together the ESP32 DevKit, an ECG front end, a pulse oximeter, a 16-bit ADC and an environmental sensor. It is an educational prototype, not a certified medical device.

## Key Components

| Component | Ref | Role |
|---|---|---|
| ESP32-WROOM DevKit (30-pin) | ESP1 | Microcontroller, I²C host and Wi-Fi web server |
| AD8232 ECG module | U2 | ECG front end; output to ADS1115 A0, lead-off to GPIO 18/19, shutdown from GPIO 5 |
| MAX30102 module (Maxim Integrated) | U3 | SpO₂ and finger detection, I²C address 0x57 |
| ADS1115 breakout | P1 | 16-bit ADC: A0 ECG, A1 body temperature; I²C address 0x48 |
| BME280 module | U1 | Room temperature and humidity, I²C address 0x76 |
| Semitec 103AT-2, 10 kΩ NTC | R1 | Body temperature sensor, lower leg of the divider |
| 10 kΩ resistor | R2 | Upper leg of the NTC divider, to 3V3 |
| DC-005 barrel jack, 5.5 × 2.0 mm | DC1 | Supply input onto the +5 V net |
| KEMET ESK106M025AC3AA, 10 µF electrolytic | C1 | Bulk capacitor on the 5 V rail |
| 100 nF ceramic, CC1H104MC1FD3F6C10MF | C2–C7 | 5 V and 3.3 V rail decoupling, plus one per sensor module |
| M3 mounting holes | SCREW1–SCREW4 | Enclosure mounting |

## Features

- ECG waveform from the AD8232, digitized by the ADS1115 on A0, shown as a live scrolling trace with R-peak heart rate and lead-off detection.
- SpO₂ percentage with finger detection from the MAX30102.
- Body temperature from a 10 kΩ NTC divider on ADS1115 A1, converted with the Steinhart beta equation (B = 3950).
- Room temperature and relative humidity from the BME280.
- One shared I²C bus on GPIO 21/22 for the ADS1115 (0x48), BME280 (0x76) and MAX30102 (0x57); the ADS1115 runs at gain ×1 and 128 SPS.
- Web server on port 80; the browser polls two JSON endpoints and draws everything client-side.
- Patient record kept in browser storage: name, ID, bed, ward, age, blood group, gender, contact and notes.
- Data logging at a 2 s to 60 s interval, up to 500 rows, with Excel (.xls) export, plus rule-based alerts with suggestions.

## Hardware Design

### Power

A 5 V to 9 V supply enters through DC1 onto the +5 V net, which feeds the ESP32 VIN pin. C1 (10 µF) and C2 (100 nF) sit on the 5 V rail and C3 on the 3.3 V rail. All sensors run from the DevKit's on-board 3.3 V regulator, each with its own 100 nF decoupling capacitor (C4 BME280, C5 AD8232, C6 ADS1115, C7 MAX30102).

### ECG path

The AD8232 output pin feeds ADS1115 A0, while its LO+ and LO− lead-off outputs go directly to GPIO 18 and GPIO 19. Its shutdown pin is driven from GPIO 5 and held high to enable the chip. GPIO 5 is an ESP32 strapping pin, but it is only driven as an output after boot. Electrodes (RA, LA and RL reference) connect through the AD8232 module's 3.5 mm jack.

### Body temperature

R2 (10 kΩ) from 3V3 and the 10 kΩ NTC (R1) to ground form a divider whose midpoint, TEMP_SENSE, goes to ADS1115 A1. Firmware converts the voltage to NTC resistance and then to temperature in °C.

### PCB

Two-layer through-hole board designed in EasyEDA Pro with all components on the top side. The outline is 88.5 × 72.3 mm, with four M3 holes spaced about 82.6 × 66.4 mm for enclosure mounting. No SMT assembly is needed.

## Outcome

The project folder contains the schematic PDF, editable EasyEDA Pro project (.epro2), Gerber ZIP, BOM, pick-and-place file and STEP model, along with the Arduino firmware (sketch plus embedded dashboard page) and a full documentation book as PDF and HTML. The Gerber ZIP includes top and bottom copper, solder mask, silkscreen, board outline, drill files and a flying-probe test file.

Documented limitations: the sketch requests 125 Hz ECG sampling, but single-shot ADS1115 conversions on an I²C bus shared with the MAX30102 make the real rate lower and uneven, which is adequate for heart-rate display but not for diagnostic ECG morphology. Heart rate uses a simple threshold detector, body temperature is an uncalibrated contact reading, the web server has no authentication or HTTPS, and Wi-Fi credentials are hard-coded. Listed next steps are WiFiManager provisioning, MQTT or cloud publishing, flash storage for logs, battery power with charging, and an enclosure.
