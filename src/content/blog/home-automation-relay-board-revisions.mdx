My home automation boards bring Wi-Fi and wall-switch control to household mains loads such as lights, fans and sockets. Each one is a through-hole PCB built around an ESP32 module that runs its own web server, so it needs no cloud service, no smartphone app and no subscription: only the local Wi-Fi and a browser.

The design went through three versions. Here is what stayed the same and what changed.

## The shared core

All three boards are built on the same ideas:

- **Mains power on the board.** A Hi-Link AC-DC module powers the board straight from 220 V, followed by an AMS1117-3.3 LDO. No external adapter is needed.
- **An isolated drive per channel.** Each relay has a PC817C optocoupler, a 2N2222A driver, a 1N4007 flyback diode and a status LED. Relays switch on when their GPIO goes high.
- **Physical switches still work.** Every relay has a wall-switch input, and the firmware debounces each input over 50 ms.
- **USB-C programming** through a CH340C USB-to-UART bridge.

## V1: four channels

The first board has four SRD-24VDC-SL-C relays on a 101.6 × 81.5 mm PCB, powered by an HLK-10M05T module. It has USBLC6-2SC6 ESD protection on the USB-C port and a 4-pin XH expansion header.

## V2.1: six channels and a DC input

V2.1 moves to six relays and switches to an HLK-PM01 AC-DC module. It also adds a 5 V DC jack (DC-005) as an alternative power input. At 121.6 × 97.3 mm it is the largest of the three.

## V2.2: six channels, smaller board

The latest revision keeps six relays but fits them on a smaller 110.0 × 88.9 mm board. It goes back to the HLK-10M05T module and to USBLC6-2SC6 ESD protection on the USB-C port, and has a 4-pin XH expansion header.

## The dashboard

The firmware serves a card-based dashboard on port 80 that refreshes every 3 s. Each relay can have a custom name, a daily ON/OFF schedule synced over NTP and a countdown auto-off timer. Per-relay statistics (trigger count, total ON time, last ON/OFF) and a 10-entry event log are stored in the ESP32's NVS flash.
