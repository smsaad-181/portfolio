## Motivation

A mains-powered water tank monitor for a home installation: measure level via ultrasonic sensor, trigger a relay at configurable thresholds, and report status over Wi-Fi. The HLK-PM01 module keeps the form factor small enough to fit inside a standard enclosure.

## Key Components

- **ESP32-S3** — Wi-Fi MCU running the web UI, MQTT reporting, and relay control
- **HC-SR04** — ultrasonic distance sensor; TRIG/ECHO interface, but ECHO output is 5 V
- **HLK-PM01** — compact AC-DC converter (230 V → 5 V), powering the board from mains
- **RGB LED** — color-coded status: full (blue), normal (green), low (yellow), empty (red)
- **Relay** — switches the pump/valve load

## Challenges & How They Were Solved

**Wi-Fi failure on the custom PCB** — The board powered up and firmware ran, but Wi-Fi would not initialize. After extensive firmware-side debugging (checking SDK logs, heap, antenna clearance), the root cause was traced to pull-up resistors on the boot-mode strapping pins referencing 5 V instead of 3.3 V. The ESP32-S3 samples those pins at boot; a 5 V pull-up pushed the sampled voltage above the 3.3 V logic rail, causing the wrong boot mode to be latched. Corrected to 3.3 V references; Wi-Fi initialized normally on the next power cycle.

**Unprotected 5 V ECHO line** — The HC-SR04 ECHO pin outputs 5 V, but the ESP32-S3 GPIO is only 3.3 V tolerant. Initial schematic wired ECHO directly to the GPIO, risking long-term damage. Fixed with a resistor voltage divider (10 kΩ / 20 kΩ) to bring ECHO down to ~3.3 V before it reaches the MCU.

## Outcome

Stable in long-term operation. Level readings accurate to ±1 cm over the measured range. Wi-Fi reporting and relay control functioning as designed.
